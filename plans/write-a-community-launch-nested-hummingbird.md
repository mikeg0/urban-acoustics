# Urban Quiet Initiative — Community Launch Plan

## Context

The Urban Quiet Initiative is launching publicly this week. The petition page, dashboard, and proposal at [frontend/public/quiet-initiative/](frontend/public/quiet-initiative/) commit to a **public-comment appearance at Salt Lake City Council on Tuesday, June 2, 2026** — five days from today (2026-05-28). Sign-ups have just opened; the launch story is the launch itself, not a signature delivery. The asks are concrete: 40 calibrated noise sensors on State Street from North Temple to 900 South, a 12-month phased pilot, $95K–$180K (largely grant-eligible), block-level public dashboard, and an explicit *no-enforcement* posture.

You are launching solo, with a ~$200 outreach budget, willing to speak at council and meet officials but not to run social media. This plan is built around those constraints: free channels, press leverage, and recruiting your "first 5 humans" inside 30 days.

A strategic gift you should not waste: **Council District 4 — which contains your sensor corridor — is currently vacant**. The mayor must appoint a replacement by June 11, 2026. The appointment process is your single biggest leverage point for the next two weeks.

## Strategic Reality

- **You have a public commitment, but not a "deliver signatures" commitment.** The site promises a public-comment appearance at Council on June 2 — that's keepable solo. Sign-ups are live but the on-page counter is gated and stays hidden until ≥100 real signups, so nothing on the site overstates the coalition. The plan splits into a 5-day sprint (launch well, show up at Council) and a 30-day sustain (turn the launch moment into a real coalition).
- **You are the messenger, not the movement.** Public comment from one engineer is a moment. Public comment from one engineer flanked by a physician, a State Street resident, and a community council board member is a story. Phase 1 priority is recruiting the *first five humans*.
- **The D4 vacancy reshuffles the math.** Your corridor straddles D3 (Wharton) and D4 (vacant). Whoever the mayor appoints in D4 will be a council member by your launch window. That appointment is the most actionable advocacy target in the entire plan.
- **The health frame is your strongest tone.** The site already leads with sleep, cardiovascular risk, and productivity — not "we are annoyed." Keep it. Resist any temptation to drift into enforcement language; it would alienate the demographic (renters, immigrants, motorcyclists, vehicle owners) you need.
- **Reddit is the free channel that matters in SLC.** r/SaltLakeCity is unusually high-signal for local civic causes, and posting an explainer there is closer to writing a forum post than running social media. Treat it as one of the press targets, not as "social."

## The 30-Day Sustain Plan (June 3 – July 2)

Delivery is a moment. Council motion is the goal. The sprint produces the moment; the sustain plan converts it.

1. **Week 1 — D4 vacancy advocacy.** The mayor's appointment deadline is June 11. Write a public letter to the mayor (post on the site, send to press) outlining the criteria for a D4 appointee that would represent corridor residents on this issue — without naming a candidate. If credible names surface, brief each one privately on the proposal so they can speak to it. A D4 appointee who lists the pilot as a priority on day one is worth more than 5,000 additional petition signatures.

2. **Week 1–2 — Community council appearances.** Get on the agenda at Central City (closest fit), Liberty Wells, and the Ballpark neighborhood meeting. 5-minute presentation, ask for a board resolution of support. These resolutions are the cheapest, most legitimate form of local endorsement you can collect.

3. **Week 2 — One-on-ones with sitting council members.** Especially Wharton (D3), Carlsen (D5, Vice Chair), Puy (D2, Chair). 30 minutes each, walk through the dashboard live. Goal: identify which one will sponsor a motion to direct staff to scope the pilot.

4. **Week 2–3 — Recruit a coalition lead.** You said you'll speak at council but not run social. That means by July you need a non-you human who runs neighborhood outreach. Most likely sources: someone you meet at a community council meeting, a UPHE volunteer, a State Street resident who signs up via the form. Treat this as a hire, not a hope — write a one-page "what this role does" and ask every ally to forward it.

5. **Week 3 — UPHE formal endorsement.** Once you have an interested physician, push for an org-level letter to council. UPHE letters land.

6. **Week 3–4 — Grants and budget.** Begin drafting one grant application as proof to council that you can fund the pilot externally. *Bloomberg Philanthropies Mayors Challenge* and *EPA Environmental and Climate Justice Community Change Grants* are the two listed in the proposal — pick one and draft. The drafting itself becomes a credibility artifact in council conversations.

7. **Week 4 — Public progress update.** A blog post / explainer on the site: "30 days since delivery — here's where each council member stands, here's what's next." Drives the council member tracker on the site (already built) and rewards everyone who signed.

## Verification — How You Know the Launch Worked

This is community organizing, not code. There are no tests; there are signals. Watch these:

- **June 2 day-of:** Did public comment happen? Did the packet land on every council member's desk? Did at least one reporter publish?
- **+72 hours:** Did any council member's office reply requesting a briefing? Did the petition signature count tick up? (It should — the press hits drive it.)
- **+2 weeks:** Did the mayor's D4 appointment go to someone who has been briefed? Did Central City Neighborhood Council put you on their agenda?
- **+30 days:** Do you have *named* (not aspirational) allies in at least 3 of the 5 categories? Is there one council member willing to be quoted as supportive? Have you secured a coalition lead recruit?
- **+90 days:** Is there a council motion on the agenda directing staff to scope the pilot? If not, what changed in the political math and what's the new ask?

If any of those signals turn red, the response is *not* to push harder on the same channel — it's to find the unblocker. Most often the unblocker is a real human ally you haven't met yet, not a better press pitch.
